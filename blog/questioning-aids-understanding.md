title: Questioning aids understanding
date: 2026-10-21
---

The strongest evidence is for prompting a learner to explain _why_. It isn't for any one named framework. A [meta-analysis of self-explanation prompts](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-018-9434-x) (Bisra et al., 2018) found a moderate effect across 64 reports (g = 0.55). Explaining for yourself beat being handed an explanation (g = 0.35). A meta-analysis of critical-thinking instruction (Abrami et al., 2015) found a smaller overall effect (g+ = 0.30). Teacher-posed questions were a little higher (0.38).

Socratic, five whys and Toulmin are useful vocabularies for the kinds of question. I found no direct experiments testing their taxonomies. Five whys has been criticised outright (Card, 2017)because it forces a single causal chain (which sucks, of course). One case Card describes had about 30 causal pathways and over 75 "whys". Five whys would cover under 3% of them.

The caveat that matters most for this blog: gains come when the learner generates the answer. If a "why" link goes straight to my answer, the reader skips that step and probably gets less out of it. One option is to show the question first and let readers guess before they click.
