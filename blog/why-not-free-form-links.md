title: Why not free-form links
date: 2026-10-21
---

Wikis, [Zettelkasten](https://zettelkasten.de/introduction/) and backlink tools already join notes with plain links. They work well enough. The weakness is that a bare link says two notes are related, not _how_. You have to guess whether the target defines the claim, supports it or attacks it. A typed question states the relationship before you click.

The evidence that this helps is small and mixed. Zhao's [1995 thesis](https://oro.open.ac.uk/56455/) ran three small studies, with roghly 25 people each. Visible link types helped only in the exploratory third one, and only for recalling how ideas were structured (which is also what we hope to achieve). Her second study suggested that readers with a specific goal didn't need the labels. Baron et al. (1996) found no browsing difference, though labelled links helped on a query task.

Typing has a cost too. Some research* (very obviously) finds that having too many types is counter-productive for users. People resist making structure explicit too. This is why Because limits the types of questions you can ask, and why a plain `[[link]]` in the body is still allowed (unless you want to come up with a new strategy).

Some research:
- Conklin and Begeman [gIBIS](http://www.gerrystahl.net/teaching/winter12/Conklin_gIBIS.pdf)
- Shipman and Marshall's "Formality Considered Harmful" (1999)
