because: publish
title: Feedback rarely reaches authors
date: 2026-10-21
---

Give readers a place to leave step-level feedback and they will use it. It rarely changes the work, though. In a study of 350 BMJ research papers, 105 drew substantive criticism. Authors replied to [47 of them (45%)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2919680), whatever the severity. On Stack Overflow, about three quarters of comments on answers are informative (Zhang et al., 2021). They are rarely folded back into the answer. [Another analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07848) found about half of comments asked for an update. Only a third of the requests that were addressed led to an edit.

Structure and moderation help. On [Kialo](https://www.kialo-edu.com/), every claim is a node and revisions are moderated. [93% of sampled revisions](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.10250) improved the claim. Open annotation without the author's consent goes badly. This famously went wrong for Genius, which let anyone annotate on any post, but did not allow authors to report annotations until later. Authors could not opt-out, were harassed, and the community had to build scripts to circumvent the situation.

So the bottleneck looks like author incentive, not the channel. A Because note can't make authors respond. But an unanswered question shows up as a gap, so ignoring a criticism becomes a public choice. And readers get a precise place to point.
