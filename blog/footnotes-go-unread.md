title: Footnotes go unread
date: 2026-10-21
---

Articles can already show part of the path. Footnotes, appendices and supplementary files do it. The trouble is that readers rarely open them. A study of Wikipedia found only [0.29% of page views](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3366423.3380300) lead to a click on a reference.

Supplements are a mixed picture. In a [survey of authors, reviewers and readers](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6157527) (14% response), people said they read them often. An [editorial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4630891) argues they are rarely reviewed and poorly linked to the main text. Conklin's 1987 hypertext survey called footnotes and similar devices "rough tools at best".

There's a second limit, and it has nothing to do with uptake. A footnote holds _material_: a source, a caveat, a table. It doesn't hold the question that made the author go and find it. And it sits in the order the author chose. You still can't tell which objections were considered.

This weakens my own case too. If readers don't open footnotes, they may not click question links either. What I'm betting on is that a question shows up on the page as a prompt, not a superscript number. I haven't seen evidence either way yet.
